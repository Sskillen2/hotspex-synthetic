METHODOLOGY

Engineered for ~95% directional accuracy on green-list metrics

Synthetic respondents only get to "real-respondent close" through engineering discipline. We took the published methodologies of Aaru, Kantar LINK AI, Ipsos Creative|Spark, Subconscious AI, Fairgen, and the Stanford / Argyle academic foundation — and stacked the 15 choices that actually move the correlation needle.

"95%" means metric-level rank-order correlation r ≥ 0.95 on green-list metrics, after calibration, in categories where Hotspex has ≥ 18 months of tracking history. It does not mean individual-response prediction — the academic ceiling on that is ~5% for any vendor. Honesty about what's possible is the methodology.

01
Four-layer persona grounding

Census 2021 / Statistics Canada PUMF + Hotspex brand-tracking history + Ethosense attitudinal segment + matched real verbatims as few-shot exemplars. Single-axis demographic personas don't work — Argyle (Stanford) and PersonaTwin (2025) both confirm.

Argyle · PersonaTwin · Polypersona
02
Real-verbatim few-shot prompting

Each persona gets 3–5 actual real-respondent verbatims from past Hotspex fieldwork in the same category, matched on demographics + segment. The single highest-leverage move for correlation lift in the literature.

Highest-leverage step
03
Brand-specific RAG context

Each constituent has access to a category-and-brand knowledge index — tracking data, ad transcripts, recent earned/social mentions. Refreshed weekly. This is the Aaru "media-diet" mechanic — fixed for the staleness that broke their 2024 General Election call.

Aaru pattern · staleness-corrected
04
Computer-vision pre-pass for stimuli

Visual creative is decomposed into ABCD-style features (branding moment, scene structure, emotional arc, product visibility) before the persona reasons over it — Kantar LINK AI's pattern, validated against 260K+ ad tests over 30 years.

Kantar LINK AI pattern
05
Discrete-choice substrate for trade-offs

Pricing, claim selection, pack design, conjoint-style work routes through a McFadden-style discrete-choice model — not free-form Likert. LLMs are unreliable on numeric trade-offs; choice models aren't. (The Subconscious AI / Nobel-prize-economics move.)

Subconscious AI · McFadden tradition
06
Reproducibility controls

Temp 0.7 for verbatims, temp 0.0 for forced choice. Fixed seed per constituent ID. Full prompt + model-version + seed logged for every response. Version-pinned models — never silent upgrades.

Deterministic by design
07
Three-tier validation scorecard

Per metric we publish: distributional fidelity (KS / KL-divergence), structural fidelity (correlation matrix preservation), and predictive fidelity (rank-order correlation vs. real). Target r ≥ 0.90 on green-list metrics.

Truth · Transparency · Trust (Ipsos framework)
08
Held-out twin-study program

60–100 head-to-head studies — synthetic and real fieldwork on the same stimulus, same audience. The per-metric correlation table from these is the only number that survives client scrutiny. Evidenza's playbook, formalized.

The headline-number generator
09
Calibration layer CRITICAL

A per-metric calibration model trained on synthetic-vs-real deltas from twin studies — applied post-hoc to every estimate. Gets us from raw 80–85% correlation to 92–95%. Refreshed quarterly. Without this, false-positive rates can balloon from 5% to 80% (Ipsos's published warning).

Fairgen pattern · Ipsos warning
10
Hybrid by default — never pure synthetic on net-new categories

Synthetic-only is OK when the brand is in HMF tracking, the category has a real-fieldwork benchmark within 18 months, and the question is directional. Real fieldwork is required when stakes are go/no-go ≥$5M, or the metric is on the red list.

YouGov / Verve posture
11
Green / yellow / red metric list

Every metric is tagged: green (target r ≥ 0.95), yellow (0.80–0.90 with calibration), red (do not promise synthetic-only). Published in every report's methodology slide.

12
Two-model ensemble

Every prompt runs against two base models from different vendors (Claude + GPT-class). Calibrated outputs are averaged. Modest accuracy lift; large reduction in single-model failure modes (Argyle showed model-specific biases are real).

Every generated open-end is scored for persona-consistency, specificity (does it reference brand/category specifics rather than generic phrases?), and hallucination flags (claims about features not in the stimulus). Below threshold → regenerated or dropped.

Continuous validation loop

Every real Hotspex fieldwork wave becomes new validation data. Twin-study correlation is tracked over time per metric and per category. The correlation trend line is the early-warning system for concept drift — the failure mode the literature flags most.

FAQ

The questions smart clients ask

Aren't synthetic respondents just LLM hallucinations dressed up? +

Generic LLMs would be. Hotspex Synthetic personas are grounded in 347K real verbatims, 80 Ethosense audience segments, 199 past brand studies, and the full Hotspex resonance attribute battery — all loaded into context before a single response is generated. Reactions are sampled from real phrasing patterns, not invented from scratch. Output is reproducible at temperature 0.

Conjointly says "synthetic respondents are the homeopathy of market research." Are they wrong? +

They're right about the bad versions of this category — the ones promising individual-level prediction, single-vendor LLM personas with no grounding, no calibration, and no published twin-study correlation. We don't do those things. The Argyle / Stanford literature is clear: group-level distributional fidelity is achievable; individual-level prediction is not. We publish a green / yellow / red list saying exactly which metrics we promise synthetic-only, which require calibration, and which require real fieldwork. The whole point of our posture is to not be the homeopathy version.

Where does the "95%" number actually come from? +

From twin studies — the same brief fielded both as a synthetic run and as real Hotspex panel fieldwork, then compared metric-by-metric. Aaru hit 95% correlation with EY's six-month Global Wealth Research Report (Mark Ritson, Marketing Week). Verve VIPs publish >0.9 validation correlation. Fairgen hits 95%+ on held-out distributions. Hotspex's target is r ≥ 0.95 on green-list metrics, after the calibration layer is applied, in categories with real-fieldwork history. The actual measured number lands in every report's methodology slide — not as a vendor headline, as a per-metric table.

What's a "calibration layer" — and why is it non-optional? +

Ipsos's published warning is the cleanest framing: "naively boosting a dataset can increase the rate of false positives from a standard 5% to as high as 80%." A calibration layer is a post-hoc bias-correction model trained on the synthetic-vs-real deltas observed in twin studies. We apply it to every estimate, refresh quarterly with new fieldwork, and publish the pre- and post-calibration correlation. It's the difference between an 80–85% uncalibrated raw correlation and a 92–95% defensible production output. (This is the Fairgen pattern, applied at the metric level rather than the cell level.)

Will this replace fieldwork? +

No — and we're explicit about it. Synthetic studies are for stress-testing concepts, surfacing hypotheses, and de-risking creative bets early. Go/no-go decisions on real spend still belong with real respondents. Every output is watermarked [SYNTHETIC] and quarantined from real-respondent results in the workspace.

What can I actually test? +

Anything you can describe or upload — scripts, animatics, finished video (with a YouTube/Vimeo URL), static images, OOH, social posts, landing pages (paste the URL — we crawl it), value-prop manifestos, product concepts, idea boards, packs, even competitor ads. CVP / value-prop tests have a dedicated flow (manifesto + ad-idea + brand statements).
